For the centered moments $u_t=g_t(\hat\gamma)-\bar g_T(\hat\gamma)$,
define $\hat c_\ell=(T-\ell)^{-1}\sum_{t=\ell+1}^T u_tu_{t-\ell}$.
Using the Bartlett plug-in constant from Equation (2.2) of
\href{https://doi.org/10.2307/2297912}{Newey--West (1994)},
with the autocovariance divisor and ceiling rounding specified in the prompt, we set
\[
  m=\left\lceil4(T/100)^{2/9}\right\rceil,\quad
  s_0=\hat c_0+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\hat c_\ell,\quad
  s_1=2\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\ell\hat c_\ell,
\]
\[
  C=1.1447\left[(s_1/s_0)^2\right]^{1/3},\qquad
  L=\left\lceil CT^{1/3}\right\rceil.
\]
Here $m=7$, $C=2.4566$, and $CT^{1/3}=25.5801$, giving $L=26$.
Thus the lag is data-dependent, with $m$ serving only as the pilot truncation.
With Bartlett weights $w_\ell=1-\ell/(L+1)$,
$\hat S=\hat c_0+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_\ell\hat c_\ell$.
The pilot and final lags are rounded upward, and each autocovariance
uses its $T-\ell$ available products. The calculation retains the
centered-moment implementation without prewhitening or a separate
degrees-of-freedom adjustment.
