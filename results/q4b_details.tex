The estimation uses 1,129 monthly observations of annual real returns, from December 1927 through December 2021.
The return construction and ending-month alignment are identical to Question 3(e).
The sample mean wealth return is $\bar R_w=1.087734$.
The fitted SDF is $\hat M_t=1-1.935628(R_{w,t}-\bar R_w)$,
equivalently $\hat M_t=3.105449-1.935628R_{w,t}$; its unrounded sample mean is 1.

The coefficient is calculated analytically with the identity weighting matrix.
HAC estimation uses the specified adjusted series
$\tilde g_t=g_t(\hat b)+\hat b\hat\mu(R_{w,t}-\bar R_w)$ to account for estimation of $\bar R_w$.
The common lag is selected from the sum of all 21 centered adjusted series.
The pilot is $m=7$, the NW94 constant is $C=2.4458$,
and $L=\lceil CT^{1/3}\rceil=\lceil 25.4678\rceil=26$.
Bartlett autocovariances use divisor $T-\ell$.
The full HAC matrix is saved in \texttt{results/q4b\_hac\_matrix.csv}.
Pricing-error tests account for estimation of both $b$ and $E[R_w]$; the joint test uses 20 degrees of freedom.
The unrounded joint-test $p$-value is approximately $6.495\times10^{-38}$.

For reconstructed annual real RF minus $e^{rf_t}$, the mean signed difference is
-0.000003, the mean absolute difference is 0.000012, the RMSE is 0.000037,
and the maximum absolute difference is 0.000203.
Their correlation is 0.999999520.
No identity of the risk-free benchmarks or inflation deflators is assumed.
